ICRA 2027 Workshop

From Skills to Complex Manipulation: Grounding, Learning and Control

Most of the complex manipulation tasks a human performs can be decomposed in a sequence of primitive non-prehensile and grasping manipulations, like shelf replenishment, stacking, retrieving objects from clutter etc. For real environments and multi-tasking, there still exist many barriers and research gaps concerning efficient and reliable non-prehensile manipulation control and cognitive models facilitating the combination and sequencing of those actions. The right level of structure is not clear at the moment. This gap identified between individual manipulation skills and reliable execution of complex tasks is the main motivation for this workshop.

About

Recent years have produced significant advances in robotic object manipulation on two fronts. On the one hand, task and motion planning methods enable robots to reason about complex manipulation objectives, generate efficient action sequences, and, in some cases, explicitly account for uncertainty. On the other hand, low level control, optimization and learning methods are able to accomplish increasingly complex tasks, exploiting richer feedback to adapt to their environment.

However, a significant gap remains between those two fronts. In order for a semantically meaningful complex manipulation plan to be robustly executed, it must account for the robot embodiment, as well as to adjust to multiple uncertainties on the environment dynamics, contact type, impact dynamics etc. Moreover, low level controllers can provide information regarding feasibility and interaction, which can be exploited by the high-level decision making. Bridging the gap between those levels, enables high level systems not only to employ modern complex manipulation controllers in their task sequences as well as be able to decompose tasks to the appropriate manipulations required to achieve them by sequential execution.

The challenge increases as manipulation does not concern a single fixed based robot operating in a structured environment, but rather mobile bimanual or unimanual systems operating in diverse, dynamic workspaces. To address this, representations need to capture manipulation objectives at the appropriate level of abstraction, while remaining grounded with respect to the possibly complex and evolving environment, to the capabilities, kinematics and constraints of the robot the plans are being deployed and so on. Such grounding is essential for abstract task representations and high-level plans to be converted into feasible and robust actions, while enabling adaptation and transfer across different embodiments and environments.

ICRA 2027 provides the perfect venue for discussion of such topics, as it brings together researchers working across robot control and learning, planning, manipulation, embodied AI and many related areas. The workshop will offer opportunities to discuss how the different layers of abstraction can be brought together and be efficiently combined to create a complete system, paving the way to close the gap between those areas.

About the organizers and contact

Topics

Topics of interest

We welcome work on any part of the path from individual skills to complex manipulation, including:

  • Unimanual or bimanual mobile object manipulation
  • Non-prehensile object manipulation
  • Simple grasping and manipulation with external dexterity
  • Manipulation in clutter
  • Manipulation in constrained spaces
  • Shelf replenishment
  • Large object manipulation
  • Manipulation planning
  • Skill composition and long-horizon reasoning
  • Multimodal perception and grounding
