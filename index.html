ICRA 2027 Workshop

From Skills to Complex Manipulation: Grounding, Learning and Control

Robots can now learn to grasp, push and place objects reliably. Chaining those skills into long, contact-rich tasks is still an open problem. This full-day workshop brings together researchers in perception, robot learning and control to work out what it takes to get from single skills to complex manipulation.

Scope

Skills keep getting better. Long tasks are still hard.

Learned policies, large pretrained models and better hardware have made single manipulation skills far more capable. A robot can now be taught to grasp unfamiliar objects, open a drawer or fold a towel from a modest number of demonstrations.

Real tasks are rarely a single skill. Assembling a product, clearing a table or loading a dishwasher means picking the right skill at the right moment, adapting it to objects the robot has never seen, and keeping contact under control when things go wrong. Small errors compound over long horizons:

A skill that succeeds 95% of the time finishes a twenty-step task only about a third of the time. (0.9520 ≈ 0.36)

This workshop looks at that gap through three lenses: how skills are grounded in perception and language, how they are learned and composed, and how they are controlled through contact. We want to bring the robot learning, task and motion planning, and control communities into one room to compare approaches and agree on what is still missing.

Three threads

One session for each part of the title

Each session has a keynote, an invited talk and spotlight presentations of accepted papers. The day ends with a panel that brings the three threads together.

01

Grounding

Connecting skills to the world

Which skill applies, to which object, and when? Perception, scene understanding and language that tie abstract skills to the objects in front of the robot.

  • Open-vocabulary perception
  • Affordances
  • Language-conditioned skills
  • Scene graphs

02

Learning

Acquiring and composing skills

How are skills learned from demonstrations, play or simulation, and how are they sequenced and reused for tasks nobody demonstrated end to end?

  • Imitation learning
  • Reinforcement learning
  • Vision-language-action models
  • Skill libraries

03

Control

Executing through contact

How do long, contact-rich executions stay stable, safe and recoverable when the world pushes back?

  • Impedance and force control
  • Dexterous and bimanual manipulation
  • Failure recovery
  • Safety

Topics

Topics of interest

We welcome work on any part of the path from individual skills to complex manipulation, including:

  • Skill discovery, representation and skill libraries
  • Composing and sequencing skills for long-horizon tasks
  • Task and motion planning with learned skills
  • Vision-language-action and other foundation models for manipulation
  • Grounding language, goals and instructions in perception
  • Affordances and object-centric representations
  • Imitation learning from demonstrations, video and play
  • Reinforcement learning and sim-to-real transfer
  • Contact-rich and dexterous manipulation
  • Force, impedance and whole-body control
  • Bimanual and mobile manipulation
  • Failure detection, recovery and safe execution
  • Benchmarks, datasets and evaluation for long-horizon manipulation
